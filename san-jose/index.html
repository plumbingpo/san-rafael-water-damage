Where Water Damage Starts in San Jose Properties

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around San Jose, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and San Jose properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
