Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in San Rafael

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Do not think of it as clean water. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Stored Belongings

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

What We Do

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.
